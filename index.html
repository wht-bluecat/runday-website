Your only job was the recording.
the actual problem

You made an hour of content. One thing went out.

Recording is the part you are good at and the part you enjoy. Everything after it is a second job, and it is the job that decides whether anybody sees the first one.

The episode is the only asset

A 55 minute video is one impression on one platform. The ten pieces that could have come out of it never get made, because making them is a week of work you do not have.

Cadence breaks before quality does

Shows do not usually die of bad episodes. They die of a skipped week, then two, because post-production stacked up while you were running the business.

One wrong frame costs the channel

In this industry a thumbnail, a word in a tag or a plant in shot can throttle a video or a whole channel. A general editor does not know that. It is not in their brief.

every single week

What actually lands in your folder.

Not a scope of work written to sound big. This is the delivery list, counted.

01

The episode

  • Full long-form cut, filler and retakes removed
  • Audio cleaned, levelled and mixed
  • Multi-track sync when you record remote guests

Five verticals

Most editors have never worked a regulated vertical. We have not worked anything else. These are not hypotheticals, they are last month.

asset generation

The tools refuse the prompt

Image generators block cannabis-coded prompts outright. Every graphic gets reframed as generic horticulture so it renders at all, without the asset looking like it was censored.

per-platform policy

One cut is not always enough

What passes on one network gets suppressed on another, and the rules move. When a Short needs a different visual for a different platform, it gets one, rather than the whole thing being quietly held back.

claims

A sentence can hold a launch

Guests say things that need checking before they are published, and finding that out on launch day is expensive. The transcript gets read for it, and the flag comes to you early enough to act on.

scheduling

A week that plans for review

If your legal or brand review is part of the process, the schedule is built around it instead of colliding with it. You keep the final call on everything that publishes.

who this is for

Is this you?

We are a fit if

  • You sell into commercial growing: equipment, nutrients, lighting, environmental control, genetics, consulting
  • You already record, weekly or close to it, and post-production is the bottleneck
  • Your channel operates under platform rules that punish a wrong frame or a wrong word
  • You want one person accountable for the whole week, not four freelancers to coordinate
  • You are comparing this against a hire, not against an Upwork rate

We are not a fit if

  • You want clips only, and cheap. A $29 tool does that now and does it fine
  • You have not recorded anything yet. Come back when you have three episodes
  • You need someone to book guests and write the show. We produce, we do not program
  • You want promises about downloads or revenue. We control what ships, not what an algorithm does with it
  • You need more than two shows started this quarter. We are small on purpose
